Playable Factory · GCC · November 2026

An ad people play is easy to sell. The format isn't.

A plan for putting non-gaming playables in front of GCC brands — and the first pieces of it, built rather than described. Five of the playables on this page are real. Play one.

Mahmoud Anwar · Business Development, GCC & Egypt
Prepared for Nil Zor and Baran Aybars, following the GCC sales conversation.

01 · THE DIAGNOSIS

Nobody in the room owns the format.

In the GCC, playables are bought almost entirely out of gaming user-acquisition budgets. That is a solved sale. The non-gaming sale is not, and the reason is structural rather than creative.

For a brand here, the money sits in two places, and neither one has a reason to buy a playable:

Brand and marketing

Plans in video, out-of-home and influencers, through a media agency in Riyadh or Dubai. A playable arrives looking like production, not media — a line item with no plan to sit in.

Growth and performance

Buys on Meta, Google, TikTok and Snap against a CPA. A playable arrives looking like a creative experiment with uncertain inventory and a bigger build cost.

So the format does not lose the argument. It never gets into one. Every conversation ends with genuine interest and no budget line, and that is the thing a GCC hire has to fix first.

02 · THE FIX

Stop selling playable ads.

Sell two different things to two different buyers, and never put both in the same deck.

To the growth team

A performance creative that beats their best-performing video on cost per result. Nothing about games, nothing about engagement.

The proof
A pilot measured on their own numbers, against their own current best creative.
The first ask
One creative slot in an existing campaign — not a new budget.
To the brand team

An owned, interactive asset for a season — a Ramadan game, a National Day game — that lives in the app and in social long after the media stops.

The proof
Migros One: two million coupons redeemed in a quarter on zero media spend.
The first ask
A slot in next season's plan, pitched eight to ten weeks ahead.

The second one is the opportunity. It is where the budgets actually are, it is the harder sale, and as far as I can tell nobody in the region is making it.

04 · THE NUMBER NOBODY CAN AGREE ON

A playable costs more to build and less per result.

Every published benchmark for this format comes from a company that sells the format, and the claims run from +32% conversion lift to +319%. Quoting any single figure to a media buyer starts an argument instead of a meeting.

So I stopped quoting one. This calculator hands them a model instead: their budget, their market's CPM, their production costs, and the spend at which the extra build cost pays itself back. Every default is sourced and every one is editable, including the lift.

Where does it flip?

Open the break-even calculator. At its sourced defaults — Liftoff's conservative +32%, AppAgent's build costs, GCC CPMs — the crossover lands around $4,375 of media spend.

05 · THE ENGINE

Thirty accounts, thirty playables.

The outreach motion is the thing that makes this different from any other agency approach, and it is only possible because the build is cheap and fast.

  • Pick 30 priority accounts across delivery, fintech, telco, tourism and retail — named, with a reason each.
  • Build a spec playable for each one, branded, fifteen seconds, Arabic. Send it as a link and a short screen recording.
  • Lead with the thing, not the meeting. The email says "we built this for you, it works, here it is" — the meeting is what they ask for.
  • Run a breakfast roundtable in Riyadh and one in Dubai for growth and marketing leads. Ten people who buy, not a hundred who browse.
  • Offer the pilot at a reduced rate against agreed KPIs, measured on their numbers, in their account.

I already hold direct contacts at four named accounts from the case study — in delivery, telco, e-commerce and banking. Those are the first four conversations, and they do not need an introduction.

06 · WHERE THE DEMAND IS

Segments, and why each one.

Delivery & e-commercetalabat, noon, Jahez, HungerStation, Careem, Namshi — highest creative volume in the region and the most ruthless about cost per order. They will test anything that moves CPA.
Fintech & BNPLTabby, Tamara, STC Pay, the digital banks — fast growth, heavy performance spend, and a product whose core idea (split a payment, earn a reward) is already a game mechanic.
Telcosstc, Mobily, Zain, e&, du — the largest brand budgets in the market and the least differentiated creative. The format is a differentiator before it is a performance tool.
Tourism & entertainmentVisit Saudi, Riyadh Season, the new destinations — enormous awareness budgets, a mandate to feel modern, and a seasonal calendar that is public a year ahead.
Retail, real estate, governmentSlower, but the government awareness campaigns in particular have budget and a genuine need for engagement rather than reach.
07 · CHANNELS

Who to go through.

Media agencies priority

Riyadh and Dubai. They hold the budgets and they decide what gets planned. One agency relationship is worth ten brand meetings, and it is the slowest thing to build, so it starts in month one.

Platform teams

Snapchat and TikTok MENA. Both have enormous Saudi reach and both want differentiated creative on their inventory — a shared interest worth formalising.

Content

Arabic case studies, a short GCC playable results report, and consistent LinkedIn posting. The point is to be the person in the region who writes about this format, so the inbound arrives pre-sold.

Events

LEAP, GITEX, Dubai Lynx, Seamless Middle East. Attending is weak; speaking is better; running a playable challenge on the stand is best, because it demonstrates the product in the only way that works.

And the one nobody outside would think of: Tamatem's existing Saudi network. The group already has relationships in this market that the non-gaming side has never used.

08 · THE CALENDAR IS THE PITCH

Seasons are planned eight to ten weeks ahead.

This region's spend concentrates into a handful of moments, and each has a date by which the plan is already locked. Selling into a season after that date is impossible, and before it is easy — which makes the calendar, not the product, the thing that sets the sales rhythm.

Ramadan 2027pitch now – DecemberThe biggest advertising season in the region, and the one where CPMs rise most — Meta CPMs across the GCC averaged around 68% above normal in 2026. If Ramadan falls in February 2027, the planning window is open today and shuts before the new year.
Eid al-Fitrpitch JanuarySold with Ramadan, as the payoff moment of the same campaign rather than a separate buy.
Riyadh Seasonpitch June – JulyRuns October to March. Entertainment and tourism budgets with a mandate to feel current — the most natural home for an owned seasonal playable.
Saudi National Day · 23 Septemberpitch JulyFixed date, national scale, and heavily brand-led. A playable with a local idea behind it travels here.
White Fridaypitch SeptemberPure performance season. This is the growth team's pitch, not the brand team's: cost per order, nothing else.

Each season gets a ready-made campaign concept built in advance, so the pitch is a working thing rather than a proposal to build one.

10 · WHAT I WOULD NEED

Four things, none of them money.

  1. Production capacity for spec builds. Thirty playables in ninety days only works if Flex can turn a branded concept around in days. I need to know the real limit before I promise it to anyone.
  2. A pricing floor I can quote. Not a rate card — just the number below which a pilot stops making sense, so I can negotiate in a room without going back for approval.
  3. Two or three non-gaming references I am allowed to name. Migros One carries a lot, but a buyer always asks "who in my sector?" and right now I have nothing to answer with.
  4. An introduction into Tamatem's Saudi network. The relationships exist inside the group; the non-gaming side has never used them.
